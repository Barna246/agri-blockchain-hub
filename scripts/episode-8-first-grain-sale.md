# Episode 8: The First Blockchain Grain Sale, Ten Years Later
Narrative episode telling the AgriDigital story in full, plus a real update on what happened to the company since. Capped under 90 seconds.

This case study has been sitting in the ledger since early in the series (idea-7), used only as a quick reference in the intro episode. This is the full story, told properly, with an honest close that actually has an answer instead of an open gap.

---

## Full script (~85 sec)

"December 2016. A farmer in Australia delivers a truck of wheat. The second it hits the scale, he's paid. Not next week. Not after an invoice clears. That second.

Grower David Whillock delivered 23.46 metric tons of wheat to a buyer in Dubbo, New South Wales. A smart contract, running on a private Ethereum instance built by a two-year-old startup called AgriDigital, triggered payment and title transfer at the same moment the grain was weighed. Normally a farmer delivers first and waits, sometimes weeks, exposed the whole time if the buyer can't pay. This closed that gap to zero.

It wasn't a one-off. In 2017, AgriDigital ran a pilot with CBH Group, Australia's largest grain exporter, at one of its mills. That December, Rabobank tested a three-party financing deal settled the same way. By 2019, the platform had roughly 1,300 active users and had processed over 1.6 million metric tons of grain.

Ten years on, AgriDigital is still running. It closed a fresh 25 million dollar funding round and pulls in real revenue every year. But blockchain isn't the pitch anymore. It's one feature folded into a bigger grain management platform. That's not failure. That's what happens when new tech actually works: it stops being the headline and becomes the plumbing."
