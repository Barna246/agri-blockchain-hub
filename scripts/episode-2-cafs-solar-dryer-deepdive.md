## Full script

**HOOK — 0:00-0:12**
"A Nigerian food research institute just plugged blockchain into a solar dryer. Not a metaphor — an actual solar-powered drying rig, with a blockchain layer tracking who uses it and when. This is the Nigeria story I teased in episode one. Let's get into it."

**THE PROBLEM — 0:12-0:40**
"Start with the number that makes this worth doing at all: Nigeria loses somewhere between 30 and 50 percent of its perishable produce every year, according to NSPRI's own Executive Director, Professor Lateef Sanni. Bad storage, weak processing, no access to preservation tech. And it doesn't hit everyone equally — smallholder farmers, women processors, and rural communities absorb most of that loss, straight out of their income."

**THE SOLUTION — 0:40-1:15**
"So here's the fix: solar-powered dryers instead of open-air drying or fuel-burning methods — cleaner, cheaper to run, and it protects produce from contamination and rain. That part isn't new; NSPRI's been building solar dryers for years. What's new is the blockchain layer on top. CAFS Africa's founder, Azeez Salawu, frames it as solving an access problem, not just a drying problem — these dryers are shared community infrastructure, and blockchain gives you a transparent, tamper-proof record of who's using the equipment and when. That's the actual use case. Not a token, not a coin — a shared-asset logbook nobody can quietly rewrite."

**WHY BLOCKCHAIN, SPECIFICALLY — 1:15-1:45**
"Remember episode one — blockchain in agriculture is usually about fixing trust and paperwork, not farming itself. This is that exact pattern, just physical instead of financial. When equipment is shared across a community, the normal failure mode is: whoever has the right connections gets priority access, usage never gets tracked properly, and there's no accountability when something breaks. Dr. Michael Omodara, who's leading training and deployment on this project, has said the blockchain piece is there for real-time monitoring and accountability. That's the whole pitch — not hype, just a fairer queue."

**WHO IT'S ACTUALLY FOR — 1:45-2:05**
"And they're not just dropping in hardware and walking away. The project includes training smallholder farmers, women processors, and young people to operate and maintain the dryers — with some of those young people trained as technicians and digital facilitators in their own communities. That's the part that actually determines whether something like this survives past the launch event."

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW YET — 2:05-2:30**
"Now the honest part, because that's the whole point of this series: at the point this was unveiled, there's no public number yet for how many dryers, how many farmers, or what the budget is. This is backed by the UNDP's Tadamon Accelerator for Food Security, and NSPRI is a real federal research institute, not a random startup — so the credibility is there. But it's a launch, not a three-year track record. I'll be watching for real numbers, and I'll bring them to you the moment they're public."

**CLOSE + CTA — 2:30-2:50**
"This is exactly the kind of project this series exists to find — local, real, barely covered anywhere in Web3 content. Next up, I'm either tracking this one for updates or going deeper into a Kenyan project doing something similar with savings instead of hardware. Follow for that."
